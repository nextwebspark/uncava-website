How to map an executive market before the kickoff call

A mandate is won or lost in its first week. A practical method for turning a role title into a defensible universe of companies and people — before the client asks.

[Author name][Date] · 8 min read
On this page Start from the role, not the company Draw the universe wide Decide on every company Map people, not just logos Walk in with a map

The first conversation with a client after they sign a mandate sets the tone for everything after it. Walk in with a job description and a promise to “start researching”, and you spend the next month catching up. Walk in with a picture of the market, and the client starts making decisions with you on day one.

Start from the role, not the company

Before a single company name, write down what the role actually needs: the scale of the business the person must have run, the ownership model they’ve worked under, the markets they know. A Group CFO for a listed energy utility and a CFO for a founder-owned renewables developer share a title and almost nothing else. The brief is the filter.

Draw the universe wide

Translate the brief into criteria a database understands — industry, headcount band, revenue band, headquarters region — and pull everything that matches. Resist the urge to pre-judge. A universe that is too narrow on day one is the reason a search stalls in week six.

Decide on every company

Every company in the universe gets one of three answers: it stays in the universe, it’s shortlisted, or it’s declined. The declines matter as much as the shortlist. When the client asks “what about the obvious competitor?”, the answer is already written down.

A declined company is research, not waste. Recording the no is what makes the yes defensible.

Map people, not just logos

For each shortlisted company, name the executives who could plausibly do the job — the incumbent in the role, the deputy, the finance director of the largest division. One company with three credible people is worth more than three companies with none, and the map should show it.

Walk in with a map

Show the client where the market is: which countries, which companies, which people. Geography answers relocation and compensation questions before they’re asked, and a shared map turns the kickoff into a working session instead of a status update.
