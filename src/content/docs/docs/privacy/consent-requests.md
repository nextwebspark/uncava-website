With **Notices and consent requests** turned on, Uncava can ask a candidate, for each purpose your firm offers, whether they agree. The candidate answers on their privacy page and can withdraw at any time. Uncava keeps each answer, along with the exact sentence the candidate agreed to.

:::note[Before you start]
An admin has to choose **Notices and consent requests** as the **Mode** in [candidate privacy](/docs/privacy/set-up-candidate-privacy) and turn on the purposes your firm asks about. Any **Admin** or **Member** can ask.
:::

## Ask for consent

1. **One person**
   In the candidate drawer’s **⋯** menu, or on the **Privacy** card, choose **Ask for consent**. Tick the purposes to ask about. The dialog shows the email word for word before it is sent.
2. **Many people**
   Select people in **Candidates** and choose **Ask for consent**. One request can cover up to 500 people. After it is sent, Uncava lists who it skipped and why.

Uncava skips a person when no privacy email may reach them: they have no email address, are marked do not contact, have processing restricted, or are on your suppression list. It also skips a person who already gave consent and isn’t due to renew, and anyone asked, or who declined or withdrew, in the last 14 days. Consent requests and privacy notices count against the same daily limit. A request also tells the person what a privacy notice would, so it marks them notified.

## What the person sees

The email links to their privacy page. It shows one **Yes** or **No** for each purpose you asked about, with neither picked. Every answer is confirmed by email. While anything they agreed to is still in force, that email carries a link where they can **Withdraw** it at any time.

:::note[Silence is never consent]
A request nobody answers closes after 30 days and changes nothing: not the lawful basis, not retention, and not what a client can see.
:::

## Record an answer given another way

If someone said yes or no on a call or in a letter, choose **Record an answer**. Pick the purpose and the answer (**given**, **declined** or **withdrawn**), and enter the day, today or earlier.

## Follow each consent

The **Privacy** card on the candidate’s **Profile** tab lists each purpose with its state:

| State             | Meaning                                                           |
| ----------------- | ----------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **Not asked**     | Nobody has asked yet.                                             |
| **Asked**         | A request is open, waiting for an answer.                         |
| **Given**         | They agreed, on their page or by an answer a colleague recorded.  |
| **Renewal asked** | Consent is still in force, and they have been asked to extend it. |
| **Declined**      | They said no.                                                     |
| **Withdrawn**     | They agreed, then took it back.                                   |
| **Expired**       | The consent ran its term.                                         |

When someone’s lawful basis is **Consent** but no consent is in force, the card says **No consent recorded.** It is a warning only. Nothing is blocked, and you decide what to do.

Three quick views in **Candidates** help you follow up:

- **Asked, no answer**: a request is still open.
- **Consent ending in 30 days**: a keep-on-file consent runs out soon.
- **Declined or withdrawn**: the person said no, or took their consent back.

## Renewal and expiry

A keep-on-file consent lasts the months the request promised (12, 24 or 36), whatever the setting is when the person answers. The set number of days before it ends, Uncava asks once more. A yes extends the consent from the day it would have ended. A no lets it run until that day. A consent that passes its end date becomes **Expired**.

While a keep-on-file consent is in force, the person stays out of your retention review list. Asking someone for consent does not, on its own, keep them any longer.
